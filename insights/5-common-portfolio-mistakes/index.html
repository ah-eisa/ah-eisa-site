5 Common Portfolio Mistakes — And How to Fix Them

Structural portfolio pitfalls including account sprawl, overlapping fund holdings, stale allocations, and inefficient asset placement — with actionable fixes.

Avoiding Pitfalls: A Guide to Smarter Portfolio Management

Even experienced investors can fall prey to common structural pitfalls that undermine portfolio performance and long-term wealth accumulation. Drawing from Morningstar's analysis, this article identifies five prevalent mistakes and offers actionable solutions to help investors optimize their portfolios for better returns, reduced risk, and improved tax efficiency.

The Five Portfolio Pitfalls and Their Solutions

1. Portfolio Sprawl Across Too Many Accounts

Mistake: Investors often accumulate numerous investment accounts (brokerage, retirement, old 401ks, etc.) over time, leading to a fragmented view of their overall financial picture. This sprawl makes it difficult to monitor asset allocation, track performance, and manage risk effectively.

Solution: Consolidate and Simplify. Where possible, merge accounts to gain a holistic view of your investments. Utilize tools or advisors that can aggregate all your holdings into a single dashboard, even if accounts remain separate. This allows for better oversight and strategic decision-making.

2. Redundant Holdings in Overlapping ETFs and Stocks

Mistake: Without a clear strategy, investors might inadvertently hold multiple ETFs or individual stocks that track the same underlying assets or sectors. This redundancy can lead to over-concentration in certain areas, negating diversification efforts and increasing uncompensated risk.

Solution: Conduct Periodic Reviews. Regularly audit your portfolio to identify and eliminate overlapping holdings. Understand the underlying assets of your ETFs and mutual funds. Focus on building a diversified portfolio with distinct asset classes and sectors rather than accumulating similar investments.

3. Holding Under-performing Funds for Too Long

Mistake: Emotional attachment or a reluctance to admit a poor investment choice can lead investors to hold onto underperforming funds for extended periods. This can significantly drag down overall portfolio returns.

Solution: Implement a Disciplined Review Process. Set clear performance benchmarks and review your funds regularly. If a fund consistently underperforms its benchmark and peers, and there's no compelling reason to believe a turnaround is imminent, consider reallocating those assets to more promising opportunities. Avoid emotional decision-making.

4. Misaligned Asset Allocation Near Retirement

Mistake: As investors approach retirement, their risk tolerance typically decreases, yet many fail to adjust their asset allocation accordingly. Maintaining an aggressive, growth-oriented portfolio too close to retirement exposes capital to unnecessary market volatility, potentially jeopardizing retirement savings.

Solution: Gradually De-risk Your Portfolio. Transition from a growth-heavy allocation to a more conservative, income-generating portfolio as retirement nears. This involves increasing allocations to fixed income and other less volatile assets, protecting capital while still providing some growth potential. A glide path strategy can be effective here.

5. Sub-optimal Tax Location of Assets

Mistake: Investors often hold assets in accounts without considering the tax implications, leading to inefficient tax outcomes. For instance, placing highly taxed assets in taxable accounts or tax-efficient assets in tax-advantaged accounts can erode returns.

Solution: Strategize Tax-Efficient Asset Location. Place tax-inefficient assets (e.g., high-turnover funds, REITs, bonds) in tax-advantaged accounts (e.g., 401(k)s, IRAs) to defer or avoid taxes on income and gains. Conversely, place tax-efficient assets (e.g., broad-market equity ETFs, individual stocks with low turnover) in taxable accounts. Consult with a tax advisor for personalized strategies.

Conclusion

By understanding and actively addressing these five common portfolio mistakes, investors can significantly improve their chances of achieving their financial goals. Proactive management, regular reviews, and a disciplined approach are key to building a resilient and high-performing investment portfolio.
